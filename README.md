# Notre Dame Giving: Donor Portfolio Targeting

A team project for the Notre Dame MSBA program that models donor gift amount and giving tenure, then uses both to build a regionally balanced portfolio of prospective donors for University of Notre Dame fundraising staff.

## Context / partner

University of Notre Dame fundraising (development). The university provided two donor "portfolio" extracts with demographics, affinity indicators (Monogram, Dual Domer, current parent, giving society), gift designations, and five fiscal years of unrestricted giving history. All data stays in this repository at the owner's request. This README describes it only at a high level.

## The question

Which donors should go into a new fundraising portfolio so that it (a) targets people likely to give more, (b) targets people likely to keep giving year over year, and (c) meets the partner's constraints of about 1,000 donors per region and a minimum share of donors aged 70.5 or older?

The working target-group criteria are written out in `target.Rmd`: younger donors (roughly ages 25 to 40), donors with traits linked to higher gift amounts or longer tenure, and fewer donors dropping off.

## Approach

1. **Cleaning and feature engineering.** Parsed dates and currency fields, recoded Y/N flags to 1/0, derived general giving-society and gift-designation categories, built age groups, and assigned donors to fundraising regions by state (with ZIP-prefix splits for CA, NV, WI and PA).
2. **Exploration.** Correlation heatmap, giving by region, state, society and month, age and tenure profiles, and ZIP-by-age heatmaps.
3. **Gift-amount model.** Reshaped giving history to one row per donor per year and predicted the next year's gift with XGBoost (earlier versions used a mixed-effects `lmer` model and a random forest). Feature importance and SHAP summaries explain the model. The model's output is a donor ranking by predicted gift.
4. **Tenure model.** Modeled consecutive years of giving with OLS, then weighted least squares (`nlme::gls` with variance weighting, compared by AIC), plus a random forest. Standardized WLS coefficients were exported as variable-importance tables and used to give each donor a weighted "tenure score."
5. **Portfolio selection.** Combined the gift-amount rank and the tenure score. Within each region the team kept the top 2,000 donors by predicted gift, then the top 1,000 of those by tenure score. They compared the predicted giving of this portfolio with the university's original new portfolio and summarized the top cities in each region.

## Key findings / deliverables

- The notes in `target.Rmd` say that, in the team's models, active giving-society membership, Monogram status and Dual Domer households are associated with longer giving tenure, and that giving-society membership and current-parent status are associated with higher gift amounts.
- **Deliverables:** a proposed donor portfolio (`our_new_portfolio.csv`), donor rankings by predicted gift, tenure variable-importance tables, a top-cities-per-region summary, and Tableau workbooks for regional and variable-importance views.
- The repository does not record final model accuracy metrics, so none are reported here.

## Tools

R (dplyr, tidyr, lubridate, ggplot2, reshape2), lme4 / lmerTest, nlme, broom.mixed, randomForest, caret, xgboost, fastDummies, forecast, Tableau.
